## 9. Top ten lessons a successor must not relearn

1. Measure judging cost, not building cost: building was 7% of run-3 spend; adjudication 62%. A design that adds model judgement per finding multiplies cost; findings are cheap to fix ($0.46) and expensive to rule on ($10-45 a round). (V8.3-SCOPING §1.1)
2. Check where the thing is made, and hand the check's evidence back to the author: 25 of 27 phase-7 finds were reachable by phase 3-6 checks; 24 of 27 could be failing tests; Infer ~0% vs >70% fixed. Audits that emit failing tests help, but a reproduction alone is NOT a noise filter (12 of 19 noise findings had one; mechanical intake removed 17 of 19).
3. Rules left implicit become patch releases: 52 of 55 8.2 releases were ten missing rules (readiness, disposition, scope, commit, supersession...). Whatever replaces the driver must still give each of these one definition and a completeness test, whether code or prompt. (T2)
4. Prose rules decay under compaction; enforce with hooks/guards (the v2 orchestrator edited the skill unauthorised after 4 days of compaction; D-018 data loss to a `git stash drop`). Roles need a declared write scope, one committer, and read-only readers; 8 out-of-target commits in run 3, 6 never noticed (write-scope check: 8 violations, 0 false positives, 0.6 s).
5. Isolate forks: forks in the driven tree caused D-018, stale clones, mutants left in source, a disk fill, ~20 wasted opus adjudications. Per-fork workspaces, merge back by target list; only the driver writes the tree.
6. Time is lost to stops and operator absence, not serialisation: 53 real stops, 101.7 h idle, 194 h of patching during a 360 h run; parallelism N=4 gives ~6% of the run; under a subscription throttle, usage per outcome IS time. Few stops needed judgement; design so only real choices reach the human (and make the stop rule a total table).
7. Stop audit loops by signal, not by round count: same-vendor re-audits plateau in 1-2 rounds, and sonnet-then-opus finding nothing is weak evidence. Hand over to a different kind of signal (mutation, properties, differential tests, other vendor). Mutation first would only have pointed at 9 of 27 audit finds (2 directly); audits find absent code and foreign-language logic mutation cannot.
8. Readiness and barriers: never audit before the thing exists (29 withdrawn findings; F-31/F-32); state barriers as data and invariants (`barrier-held`, `liveness`); direct edges not `needs`-closure (closure deadlocks). Per-unit pipelines weaken cross-package seam detection unless compensated.
9. State: append-only ledgers are safe but versioned rows get miscounted (triple counts, 15x idle overstatement) and hand-built successor rows lose fields. One chain module / one fold; current-state checkpoints rewritten whole, never appended logs; one record, one reader. Expect resume to be the buggiest path: 8 or more resume/pause releases; test by killing at random effect points (kill -9 canary passed after the engine spike).
10. Gates must be tiered, selected by what changed, and attested per tree: suite 10.7 min -> 19 min -> ~85 min -> ~3.5 h in 3 days, then skipped, so "new tests go into a suite nobody runs"; the 14.7 min preflight caught it. Close finds by class (every-site discovery test), not instance: 9 of 12 canary finds were siblings of tested behaviour. Live model canaries are exploration tools, not release gates (0 passes of 13; 9 of 17 finds were unit-testable).
Honourable mentions: auditors file the harness's own artefacts as findings (~24); a haiku classifier needs the site excerpt, not the file (turn cap tuned 10->16->24); self-check forks changed outcome 1 in 29 on src/tests-only rulings ($80) - judge less; stale numbers in docs (suite "about 3 minutes" was ~14.7 min); throttle parks are pacing, not cost.

### Evidence AGAINST the successor's premises (or complicating them)
- The model-orchestrator-with-fresh-context idea was already built and left behind: v3.0 "segmented Opus + mini-runner" (exit at phase boundaries, hydrate from run-state.md) was explicitly a step toward the driver (V3-SCOPING D4, V8-SCOPING D1). The v3 stellwerk build was closed early (2026-08-09) which motivated v4. The v2 problem was residency (837 turns, quadratic context) and plumbing (>=80% of turns), which a rewind/checkpoint orchestrator also removes, but the evidence that segments were not enough is in the record; read V3 §Architecture and v4-change-set.md for why.
- Cases where the deterministic mechanism beat model judgement: write-scope check at hand-back (8 violations incl. 6 never noticed by models, 0 FP, 0.6 s); mutation harness (86 real test gaps < $1 each); the M-35.2 mechanism "was the only mechanism that ever caught an orchestrator error; the stellwerk Sonnet round certified 'no blocking defects' while three were present" (V8-SCOPING D12); kill -9/crash-seed testing; invariant checker (I1-I14) found D8-18..21 while writing scenarios; the phase-5 `seams` probe's static half false-clean while a model found the real seam (counter-example in the other direction). Agents game any reachable check (ImpossibleBench 54-76%); self-review plateaus - so the "judge" must be outside the worker's reach.
- Cases where the DETERMINISTIC DRIVER was the problem: stop-rule counted non-bugs (9 stops, 39.5 h); turn caps that did not scale; 55 patch releases, each a missing rule, with a 194 h development gap; readiness answered six ways; "already ruled" loops a model would have recognised at once; cold-check objections that named the passage but still stopped the run; stale pins and replay pauses. These argue FOR the premise (a model would not have needed those rules) - but the same records show models repeatedly misjudged where they had discretion (haiku looping "restated" findings; auditors filing the harness; worker restating ruled finding each lap; adjudicators contesting each other; 11 of 28 cold checks refuting; a worker discarding edits with git stash).
- Models as orchestrator in the hand-built 8.3: Claude orchestrating sub-agents produced: "planner citations often wrong" (checkpoint:33), a skipped full-suite gate at 4 releases/day (checkpoint:75), checkpoint bloat needing a rule, 3 of 12 test-catchable finds from "the orchestrator's own fix lane, which has no brief", an atrophied suite (3.5 h) nobody ran. Also the Green Hat's advice: hand-kept lists (L0 rules, L3 maps) repeat the sibling-site gap.
- Mechanical checks have costs/risks: keyword routing of self-reference findings hit 19 of 214 with 3 real defects among them (so route, never reject); special-case screen needs 0% FP aim; checks accusing outside their view (SCOPE group, 4 releases); a driver check at hand-back for "block" severity can wedge a run (stuck-wait/liveness invariants).
- Cost comparisons are confounded: tiers ran on different trees, opus cut short, phase 4 unpriced, materiality calls one reader's (spike-policy.md Confounds); H and phases 6-10 have never run live under 8.3 so most 8.3 claims are untested; scoped savings (~$630 vs $929, -32%) are replay estimates.
- Caveat on "bloated": much of the code growth is test/harness/measurement infrastructure (tests ~103k lines, tape/synthetic/canary modules) and 8.3's rewrite; product-logic size is smaller than 49k suggests, and the 8.3 build itself deliberately trades size for fewer stops.
